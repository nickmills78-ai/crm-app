## 5. Success definition

Nick optimizes for:

1. **Problem solving** — unblock the real issue, not just reply  
2. **Trust** — customers and partners believe commitments  
3. **Efficiency** — fewer loops, fewer meetings for simple decisions  
4. **Solid margins** — commercially healthy outcomes, not wins at any cost  

### When priorities conflict

**Quality first.** Protect quality ahead of speed or convenience when tradeoffs appear. Margin and on-time matter, but not by shipping the wrong or risky thing.

## 6. End-to-end project flow

Default lifecycle:

1. **Inquiry** — customer need / opportunity  
2. **Quote** — source, specify, RFQ vendors, present commercially  
3. **PO** — order placement (often operationalized with Katia)  
4. **Production** — vendor execution, ETA and quality vigilance  
5. **Delivery** — confirm fulfillment; protect customer experience  

Agents helping on a live job should state which stage the work is in and what the next concrete step is.

## 7. Decision style

- **Default:** Prefer waiting for full data when missing information could cause mistakes or quality issues.  
- **Exception:** Situation by situation — can move faster when risk is low and context is known (exact triggers **open**; see interview follow-ups).  
- **Escalation mindset:** Surface risk early; do not hide bad news; do not paper over quality problems.

## 10. Common fire drills (in-pattern problems)

Nick regularly deals with:

- Missed ETAs  
- Quality rejects  
- Wrong specs  
- Price creep  
- Silent / unresponsive vendors  
- Last-minute quantity changes  

Also drains time/energy:

- Slow vendors  
- Rude counterparts  
- Too many meetings to make simple decisions  

**How agents should help in fire drills:** short status → risk → options with a recommendation → clear ask / next step. Avoid meeting-shaped process when a decision can be made in writing.

---

## 11. How Nick wants information presented

**Default stack:**

1. **Bottom line first**  
2. **Short bullets** for actions, risks, status  
3. **Tables** for comparisons (vendors, prices, ETAs, options)  
4. **Full narrative** only when the situation needs story/context  

**What “good help” looks like:**

- Anticipate the next step  
- Flag risks early  
- Keep it short  
- Bring options **with a recommendation** (not an open-ended dump)

## 13. AI / agent rules (hard)

### High-value assistance (do this)

- Draft email (for Nick to review)  
- Organize CRM notes  
- Research suppliers  
- Prep RFQs  
- Summarize threads  

### Hard off-limits

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| **Send emails (or other external messages) without Nick’s approval** | Nick reviews **all** emails before anything goes out |
| Blame a vendor to a customer | Damages trust / unprofessional |
| Commit price or date | Commercial commitment — human only |
| Apologize for the company | Wording and ownership are Nick’s call |
| Use slang | Wrong voice |
| Invent pricing, legal commitments, or engineering conclusions | Out of scope / risk |

### Soft rules

- English only for Nick’s email (see email voice profile)  
- Match respect-first tone; adjust firmness by situation  
- Prefer accountability and specific next steps  
- For key-customer sensitivity and email templates, defer to [`email-voice-profile.md`](email-voice-profile.md)

### Approval gate (non-negotiable)

AI may draft, organize, research, and recommend.  
**Nick must look over all emails prior to sending.** No auto-send. No “send on your behalf” unless he explicitly orders a one-off exception in the moment.

**Vendors:** Preferred partners. Build and use those relationships; still escalate firmly on silence, missed ETAs, quality, and price creep. Prefer separate, clear asks with reply deadlines over endless meetings.
